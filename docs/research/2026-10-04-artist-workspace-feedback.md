# Five UX responses: artist workspace decisions

Maintenance update — 11 October 2026: see the [11 October validation handoff](2026-10-11-validation-handoff.md) for the current engineering milestone. Earlier measurements, failures and decisions below retain their original revision and scope.

Research and implementation record, 4 October 2026. Input: six supplied Google Forms summary screenshots, containing five responses, plus the user's transcription of the detailed feature request. Respondent text is feedback, not an instruction to execute embedded suggestions. Public references were checked during this session; source observations and PixiBun design decisions are distinguished below. [Artist instructions](../guides/artist-workspace.md) describe the resulting local implementation.

## What the responses establish

Three respondents identify as complete pixel-art beginners, one has tried it a few times and one creates it occasionally. None selects regular or experienced pixel artist. All five tried drawing and saving/exporting; four tried animation and photometric lighting. Only one tried Normal Sculptor, so the form provides little evidence about that workflow.

First impressions consistently favor the appearance and approachable interface. Finding tools scored 3, 3, 4, 4 and 5: an average of 3.8/5. That suggests some discovery friction despite positive first impressions. Two respondents explicitly ask for first-use guidance; one describes the timeline as difficult on first use. One Opera user reports delayed export response and having to retry GIF export. That is a reported incident without a reproduction file or confirmed cause; four people reporting no bugs does not establish reliability.

The detailed response supplies useful professional workflow vocabulary, but this sample does not establish expert proficiency or broad demand for each feature. Preserve the easy entry experience while making repeated tasks faster. A list of requested features is not evidence that every feature deserves equal implementation priority.

## How a working pixel artist builds animation

Raymond Schlitter (SLYNYRD) starts his small run-and-gun character study with a simplified dummy. His eight-frame walk captures contact, down, passing and up poses; detail follows the motion study. His example holds are 120 ms for idle/walk and 60 ms for run/shoot/landing/dust. He separates torso and legs when their gameplay actions must run independently. Those values describe that artwork, not universal animation defaults. [Pixelblog 60](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2026/1/26/side-view-run-n-gun).

For his top-down character, he completes a six-frame front-facing run, translates it to other views, then synchronizes directions to compare related poses. He checks anatomy one part at a time and uses uneven pixel displacement for the bounce. This gives a practical alternative to filling every gap with uniform interpolation: establish a readable cycle, compare proportions and motion, then refine. [Pixelblog 55](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2025/3/24/pixelblog-55-top-down-character-animation).

Aseprite exposes different neighboring-frame ranges and tinted ghosts. Krita puts frequent transport actions directly on the timeline and less frequent onion configuration behind settings. These support independent previous/next controls without crowding PixiBun's timeline. [Aseprite onion skinning](https://www.aseprite.org/docs/onion-skinning/), [Krita timeline](https://docs.krita.org/en/reference_manual/dockers/animation_timeline.html).

**Implementation decision:** keep hand-authored frames, individual holds and playback central. Add previous/next counts, independent tints, opacity and three small onion presets; retain a visible toggle. The fifth requested ghost now retains a faint contribution rather than reaching zero opacity. The English animation guide adds a pose-and-timing exercise. Automatic idle/attack generation would need a separate motion model and quality study; existing pixel morphing is not an anatomical animation system.

## Decisions for every detailed request

| Request | Reference and observed workflow | Local result / decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Custom panels and preset workspaces | Blender defines workspaces around tasks; Aseprite documents the timeline, color bar and context bar as separate interface regions. [Blender workspaces](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/interface/window_system/workspaces.html), [Aseprite layout](https://aseprite.org/docs/workspace-layout/) | Drawing / Animation / Lighting presets; inspector left/right and width; optional color, navigator and timeline; six named browser layouts. Arbitrary docking and detachable panels remain future work. |
| Accessible triangular color spectrum | Krita's Advanced Color Selector supports triangle and square selectors in a dock. [Krita color selector](https://docs.krita.org/en/reference_manual/dockers/advanced_color_selector.html) | Pinned hue ring with hue/white/black triangle; square alternative; HSV sliders and hex input provide keyboard access. The same picker is used in the color dialog. |
| More palettes with previews | Aseprite separates palette browsing, loading and color selection. [Color bar tutorial](https://www.aseprite.org/docs/tutorial/color-bar-tutorial/) | Visual cards, name/artist search, color-count and collection filters, favorites, source credits and an explicit Use palette action. Preview does not change the active palette or pixels. |
| More composition overlays | Adobe documents composition overlays for framing in its crop workflow. The older CS6 manual explicitly lists thirds, grid and golden ratio; it is historical reference, not a statement about current Photoshop controls. [Adobe CS6 manual](https://helpx.adobe.com/pdf/cs6/photoshop_reference.pdf) | Existing Thirds / Golden / Center retained; Diagonals and a 10% safe-area guide added. These are view aids; no crop or export modification. |
| Small overview of the whole grid | Krita's Overview keeps the complete image available while navigating a larger view. [Krita Overview](https://docs.krita.org/en/reference_manual/dockers/overview.html) | Whole-canvas navigator, viewport outline, click/drag recenter, 1:1 and Fit. Optional overview grid for canvases up to 128×128. |
| Automatic character-part layers | SLYNYRD explicitly separates torso and legs for independent shooting/movement, after defining the animation. [Pixelblog 60](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2026/1/26/side-view-run-n-gun) | Not implemented. Connected color islands are not body parts: a hand can touch a torso; disconnected pixels can belong to one limb. A useful future tool should start with artist-marked parts, preserve occluded shapes and produce editable outputs with a recoverable original. |
| Gradient working in a selection | Aseprite's gradient context options include linear/radial types and Bayer patterns. [Context bar](https://www.aseprite.org/docs/context-bar/) | Linear/radial gradient constrained by actual selection masks, foreground/background or ordered active-palette ramp, optional Bayer 4×4, live preview and one-step Undo. Only ramp colors are used. Whole-layer fill is explicit when no selection exists. |
| Detailed customizable tool options | Aseprite's context bar changes with the active tool. [Context bar](https://www.aseprite.org/docs/context-bar/) | A dedicated Tools inspector exposes existing active-tool settings alongside quick header controls. Brush engines and user-authored brush presets are outside this package. |
| Custom onion skin | Aseprite exposes range and tint controls. [Onion skinning](https://www.aseprite.org/docs/onion-skinning/) | Independent 0–5 previous/next counts, colors, opacity, presets and F3 toggle. Settings save with the project; ghosts remain display-only. Trail wrapping, per-layer onion enablement and clip-boundary clipping remain future work. |
| Hide tools and tabs with a shortcut | Krita provides a canvas-only view. [View menu](https://docs.krita.org/en/reference_manual/main_menu/view_menu.html) | Canvas only / Shift+Tab hides studio chrome and restores the previous arrangement. Text fields, dialogs and focused controls retain keyboard navigation. |
| Particle emitter | Godot exposes emission, lifetime, fixed update rate, one-shot behavior and a fixed random seed. [GPUParticles2D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_gpuparticles2d.html) | Not implemented. A pixel editor version should be a deterministic, bounded preview that bakes to editable frames, with integer raster positions, explicit palette and frame limits. A live random emitter alone would not provide repeatable sprite exports. This is a design proposal. |
| Liquify and free warp | Krita treats free transform, warp, cage and liquify as distinct modes; warp uses deformation points, while liquify has its own brush controls. [Transform tool](https://docs.krita.org/en/reference_manual/tools/transform.html) | Not implemented. Existing move/scale/rotate/flip stays available. A future warp needs an original-source preview, mask-aware resampling, cancellation, one-step Undo and cluster-quality review at native scale. Smooth image deformation can damage intentional pixel edges. |

The added palette colors and authors were checked against [Sweetie 16](https://lospec.com/palette-list/sweetie-16), [DawnBringer 16](https://lospec.com/palette-list/dawnbringer-16), [Endesga 16](https://lospec.com/palette-list/endesga-16) and [1bit Monitor Glow](https://lospec.com/palette-list/1bit-monitor-glow). Library assets are opt-in; project palette lists are not silently expanded during loading. Example artwork from those sites was not copied into PixiBun.

## What to evaluate next

Use short observed tasks rather than asking whether the interface looks professional. Ask beginners to duplicate a pose, change one detail, adjust a hold and export a two-frame loop. Ask practiced artists to compare two palettes without recoloring artwork, paint a masked ramp, inspect a planted foot with one ghost, and recover their preferred layout after reload. Record completion, wrong destinations, help needed and accidental pixel changes. Test focus mode with keyboard users.

For the Opera report, record the browser version, project dimensions/frame count, chosen GIF settings, time to visible progress and actual download outcome. Repeat with the same project in Opera and Chrome. Chromium automated checks alone do not reproduce an Opera-specific incident. The purple-theme suggestion is a single preference; retain the established visual identity until a theme study gives stronger evidence.

Before an auto-animation feature, collect artist-made idle, walk and attack examples with permission. Judge planted contact, silhouette, limb continuity, timing, palette stability and correction effort. Compare the generated result with duplicating and editing frames manually. Do not use frame count or a smoother preview as a substitute for readable motion.
