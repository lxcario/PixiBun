# Rendering, recipes and resource ownership

The [current status](../current-status.md) records the qualifying revision and observed limits for this contract; production-host and device qualification remain separate.

## Source, display and generated output

Raster canvases live in [bufferRegistry.ts](../../src/lib/engine/bufferRegistry.ts). Store metadata, history and raster ownership must move together on supported structural actions. [compositor.ts](../../src/lib/render/compositor.ts) reads DOM canvases and registry state; it excludes grid, onion skin and selection chrome. It is overlay-free, not DOM-free or mathematically pure.

Structural history restoration, structural Undo/Redo and research-output Apply use [commitWorkspace](../../src/lib/engine/workspaceTransaction.ts). Prepare replacement canvases before committing; never mutate existing live pixels inside this boundary. It retains prior metadata, raster references and history stacks/baseline for rollback. Synchronous failure restores those owners and discards queued store notifications. On success subscribers receive the final state, once per subscriber, with the state before the batch. Subscriber exceptions are reported to the console and bounded local diagnostics; they cannot turn a committed edit into a failed edit or stop later subscribers. Ordinary store writes outside this boundary retain their existing notification semantics. This boundary does not yet wrap every editor command.

History rollback retains snapshots without cloning their raster data; baseline metadata is copied because eviction changes it. An additional conservative reservation covers retained prior history and baseline until the synchronous transaction finishes, then releases on either outcome. Memory pressure can therefore refuse a transaction before publication under the existing combined limit. Research Apply consumes its preview on success or failure; the inspector clears the corresponding reference and requires a new preview after a failed attempt.

Fresh output is the default. `targetCanvas` takes precedence; `freshCanvas: false` reuses a shared mutable canvas. Callers retaining multiple frames must use independent outputs. Copying `ImageData` with `putImageData` scales with pixel area; only selecting a stored snapshot/reference is constant-time.

Raster artwork supports fractional alpha. The legacy Clean Jaggies operation fills eligible staircase gaps with alpha 160 and averaged color; it is not a binary-alpha cleanup algorithm. The current inspector previews those changes before Apply. Alpha preservation in lighting/lenses is operation-specific and must not be generalized to every artwork-changing tool.

## Pixel history, selection and display

Drawing history defaults to 4,096 entries per direction and a combined 64 MiB retained-step budget. [pixelPatch](../../src/lib/engine/pixelPatch.ts) crops ordinary edits to before/expected-after rectangles with x/y offsets; commit still captures and compares full-layer pixels. The first pixel edit after structural history keeps the whole layer. Replay seeds each edited layer's full origin once and remains separately accounted; retained patches do not eliminate baseline or transient capture memory.

During Co-Draw, [selectiveRestore](../../src/lib/engine/selectiveRestore.ts) changes a pixel only if before/after differ and the live RGBA still equals the expected result. Differing later peer colors survive Undo/Redo. This is an RGBA test, not per-pixel author identity for equal-color overwrites, and excludes structural/created-layer restoration.

The [selection mask](../../src/lib/canvas/selectionMask.ts) remains active across tool changes and constrains Pen, Eraser, Dither, shapes, Curve, Fill/Color Replace and Normal Chisel. Transparent display uses an art-grid checkerboard when enabled, with blocks bounded to readable screen size; practice uses plain paper without changing that saved preference. Display chrome is excluded from exports.

Studio and desktop practice mount [usePlaybackClock](../../src/hooks/studio/usePlaybackClock.ts) once per workspace. It accumulates elapsed time across frame changes, applies authored holds/clip order and preview speed, caps each rAF contribution at 250 ms and suspends hidden-tab elapsed time. Catch-up may skip an intermediate displayed pose. This is separate from encoded timing and does not close M3 native scheduling qualification.

## Pixel-art rotation: RotSprite v3

RotSprite arbitrary-angle rotation (`rotSpriteContour`) rotates each structural layer of the sprite separately:

- **Silhouette:** binary coverage of a Scale8x-smoothed mask of filled cells, thresholded at 50%, followed by one-cell spike/notch cleanup. Detached pieces and enclosed holes of up to 24 pixels are re-placed when the threshold would drop them.
- **One-pixel strokes:** cells in no opaque 2×2 block skip the coverage pass. They are drawn as a graph of rotated pixel centres, bridged only where neighbours separate, so unfilled line art stays closed. L-shaped elbows and one-cell kinks are then removed.
- **Outline rings:** a uniform outline width of 0–3 px is measured from peel layers and their colours. The rotated silhouette is peeled to that width (minimum 1), and each ring cell takes the colour of the nearest source cell of the same ring.
- **Interior:** a centre-weighted vote over a Scale8x raster of interior cells only.
- **Details:** interior colour regions of up to 24 pixels, or one-pixel-thin regions of up to 256, are inpainted out of the vote. They are then re-placed as connected stamps, smallest first, with their source pixel count, or the area that lands inside a narrower rotated body. Regions enclosed only by outline stay in the vote.

Output RGBA words are copied from the source; no colour is blended or invented. Pixels with any alpha are treated as occupied, and their exact RGBA is retained. Quarter turns keep direct pixel-center mapping and circular angular distance, including just below zero; requested output bounds determine clipping. The detection thresholds are heuristics: [rotSpriteContour.test.ts](../../tests/rotSpriteContour.test.ts) checks gap-free outlines, eye pixel counts, palette, area and 2 px outline width for the PixiBun mascot across eleven angles, plus closed one-pixel line art. These fixtures do not prove artistic quality for every sprite. `rotSpriteV2` and `rotSpriteV1` remain exported, unchanged, for comparison. Subsequent alpha-over composition and normal-vector rotation have separate color semantics.

The viewport caches an integer-aligned rotated-selection raster using the chosen RotSprite or Paeth algorithm, with separate normal-vector rotation where applicable. Its cache compares the floating canvas identity and exact geometry/rotation/pivot settings, so a flipped replacement canvas invalidates the old preview. The derived normal-display cache also includes source generations/revisions, relevant layer metadata and floating canvas/transform ownership. Commit evaluates through the worker with source validation. Bounded preview/commit pixel checks do not establish universal sampling quality or parity for every transform. Direct in-place writes to an unversioned floating canvas are outside this cache ownership contract.

## Memory ownership

Timeline thumbnail placeholders use the same initial markup on the server and client. Visibility observation and the fallback for browsers without IntersectionObserver begin after mounting; server capability differences must not change hydration markup.

| Owner/boundary | Policy and exclusions |
| --- | --- |
| Raster registry | Tracks selected detached raster backing estimates |
| Timeline previews | Visible/nearby frame cells retain aspect-correct backing surfaces of at most 24 px per axis; hover previews at most 96 px. Offscreen surfaces unmount. Layer order, visibility, opacity, blend and raster revision/generation determine refresh; timing and unrelated frame edits do not redraw a preview. Backing estimates register as `previews` and release on replacement/unmount |
| Undo/Redo | Pixel steps keep only the changed rectangle, before and after (`pixelPatch.ts`); independent 4,096-step caps; combined 64 MiB retained-step budget per engine, which is the usual limit; replay baseline (each edited layer's first state plus evicted steps) accounted separately |
| Collaboration | Tracks application-owned retained sync state/queues |
| Idle scratch pool | Exact-size Float32/Int32 reuse, up to 16 per bucket and 32 MiB globally; oldest idle buffers evicted on pressure; empty buckets removed; registered in combined ledger |
| Operations | Reserve before allocation and release on completion/failure/cancellation/deadline in supported paths |
| Source-bound compute | Research previews, layer/free-transform rotation, in-between generation and normal-map generation bind to the initiating document/source through [workspaceJob.ts](../../src/lib/engine/workspaceJob.ts). Metadata/project changes cancel work through its AbortSignal; a final source revision/generation check catches raw raster changes before publication. Hook disposal cancels outstanding jobs. Successful completion removes ownership watchers; reservations remain held until the operation settles |
| Suspended practice original | Original detached raster references count as `suspendedRasters`; active practice and retained original history count once per distinct engine, including each baseline. No extra raster clone is made for route custody |
| Excluded/estimated | Checked-out scratch arrays outside separately reserved operations, JS object overhead, GPU/browser surfaces, codecs, decoder copies and caches are not a complete measured heap |

The combined ledger has a 512 MiB admission limit for estimated registered owners/reservations. Pool misses allocate; acquire clears reused buffers; release retains without clearing. Duplicate releases, partial backing views, empty and oversized buffers are refused; `clearBufferPool()` drops idle references and does not invalidate active callers. Reuse reduces allocation, not all garbage collection. Pool budgets are per JavaScript realm; a worker's ledger is not a global cross-worker heap measurement.

## Drawing previews and floating view

### Studio playback under delayed callbacks

The current [shared playback clock](../../src/hooks/studio/usePlaybackClock.ts), mounted once by each workspace, accumulates elapsed rAF timestamp differences, limiting each contribution to 250 ms. It preserves the fractional remainder, consumes multiple frame holds when necessary and publishes only the final pose for that callback. A short pose can therefore be consumed without publication after a browser scheduling stall. Hidden-page callbacks reset the timestamp rather than catching up hidden time; preview speed does not rewrite stored/exported holds.

The existing [fractional-time tests](../../tests/components/BottomTimeline.test.tsx) explicitly exercise this catch-up behavior. This describes the implementation, not a guarantee that every pose reaches the screen under load. Nibbli's stricter ordered visible-pose assertion remains unchanged. Local WebKit clock diagnosis found consistent arithmetic and fast composition alongside long rAF gaps; scheduling stability and physical display latency remain unqualified in [current status](../current-status.md).

Iso Line reads isoAngleSnap in both [shape preview and commit](../../src/hooks/viewport/useShapePointerHandler.ts). ON retains the existing projected 2:1 stepper, including cardinal drags; FREE uses the ordinary bounded getClippedLine path. Preview and commit apply the current brush footprint and drawing symmetry. The toggle defaults to ON; a projected endpoint is not the pointer endpoint.

[CenterViewport](../../src/components/layout/CenterViewport.tsx) mounts one [FloatingPreviewWindow](../../src/components/studio/FloatingPreviewWindow.tsx), outside the transformed drawing stage. The window composites artwork without drawing overlays, repaints when re-expanded, and stops its pointer-down events from entering the parent drawing handler. Its scale/backdrop controls change the view; Play/Pause shares Studio playback. This is an in-app preview, not a browser-native Picture-in-Picture API or a separate project. [Phase 3 execution (local archive)](../archives.md#record-docs-research-2026-10-02-feature-repairs-phase3-md).

## Lighting and normals

Current bulb **Off/Soft/Crisp** glints use a stylized GGX-shaped response quantized into a core and shoulder. It is not a full physically based BRDF. Legacy Blinn–Phong pill code and the small shared-light research renderer are separate modes. Preserve ink, alpha and painted-normal priority; qualify on actual artwork rather than only changed bytes.

The content-aware base combines macro-form and material-scale height; appendage boosts/cache inference can depend on light direction. Do not promise all geometry is invariant with azimuth. Dome profiles have a nonzero apex slope; blend weights and normal rounding follow the implementation. The current cel ramp uses shadow/deep factors 0.86/0.72 with lightness floors/minimum drops. Geometry heuristics, material presets and anatomical labels are estimates, not recovered physical shape/material.

`buildCelRamp()` retains its input OKLCH hue/chroma coordinates while changing lightness; RGB gamut conversion can alter realized coordinates. Optional source-shading normalization and subsequent material/glint operations are separate. Do not generalize this ramp property to every output pixel. Normal-map Bake uses the worker dispatcher; live photometric analysis and Apply remain synchronous main-thread operations in [usePhotometricEvents](../../src/hooks/viewport/events/usePhotometricEvents.ts).

The chisel encodes normalized tangent vectors using `round((N+1)*127.5)`. OpenGL convention is +Y up; light direction math uses screen +Y down where appropriate. See [normalSculptor.ts](../../src/lib/art/normalSculptor.ts) and [photometricLighting.ts](../../src/lib/ai/math/photometricLighting.ts). Minecraft topology rejects void pollution at 25%; this is PixiBun's heuristic, not a Mojang validity rule.

## Color, lenses and conversion

Image conversion uses 2×2 forward-difference gradient salience, linear-light area downsampling, OKLab median-cut centroids and Lloyd refinement. The at-most-six canvas palette scan selects representative source shades; it neither recolors artwork nor establishes exactly six source colors. Canvas dither tools and image-converter color matching have different distance metrics.

The CVD lens uses Machado–Oliveira–Fernandes severity-1 matrices on **linear RGB**: decode sRGB, multiply/clamp, encode sRGB. SVG uses `color-interpolation-filters="linearRGB"` for the same display transform. The [colorspace authors' implementation documentation](https://zeileis.codeberg.page/colorspace/reference/simulate_cvd.html) explains the linear-domain correction to earlier gamma-encoded illustrations. Matrix simulation is a design aid, not a clinical prediction or accessibility certification. Monochromacy is a Rec.709 linear-luminance grayscale aid.

Squint maps every nontransparent pixel to `#140C1E`, preserves alpha and displays it over white. It is a silhouette aid, not thresholded black/white luminance. Squint takes precedence over CVD. Both leave source/export pixels unchanged. See [cvdLens.ts](../../src/lib/color/cvdLens.ts), [SVG definitions](../../src/components/studio/ViewportLensFilters.tsx).

## Recipes, workers and publication

### Temporary PixiMotion part ownership

The first motion workflow captures the selected area, or the whole active layer when no selection exists, as a temporary part with a fresh ID, explicit project/frame/layer ownership, canvas dimensions and the source buffer's generation/revision. Rename and reorder retain that binding. Frame/layer duplication creates new cel IDs without retargeting an existing part; capturing the copy creates a fresh part. A saved rigid-part definition has a separate portable contract in [storage](storage.md); opening it validates exact source pixels and captures a fresh session binding. Runtime generations are not persisted.

Source deletion, project changes, resize, missing/replaced buffers and changed source revisions refuse bake. Locked sources refuse bake until unlocked. Refusal resolves the source without allocating a blank buffer or substituting the active cel. Supported pixel edits must touch the registry revision; unregistered direct canvas writes are outside this freshness contract. The inspector explains invalid sources and disables bake; the viewport suppresses their derived preview. A valid motion preview replaces the displayed source composite so transparent vacated pixels do not reveal the original underneath.

Baking prepares replacement source pixels and duplicated frame canvases before publication. Final source/workspace validation precedes one structural history step and publication through `commitWorkspace`. The source remains untouched on staging failure; publication failure restores the old registry, store and history. A successful bake updates Pose 0 and inserts the remaining poses after the source ID, consumes the temporary part and selects the moved layer on the last new cel. Existing later clip members keep their frame identities; clips containing the source extend to include its new poses. Undo records the actual pre-bake active cel, even when it differs from the source. Copied research references within the source frame fork to the new IDs; external references retain provenance. Existing palette region bindings copy to each new frame with fresh binding IDs; binding-limit overflow refuses staging. Palettes participate in the same Undo/Redo edit. These regions keep their original canvas coordinates: bake does not infer regions that move with a part. Review region semantics before indexed export.

The workflow admits 2–30 poses and finite settings. Capture checks memory admission before reading/copying only the captured rectangle. Static Start/End preview and interpolated playback/bake translations use the same Math.round sampling, including negative half-pixel rounding; authored settings retain their finite fractions. Zero results normalize negative zero. Finite fractional pivots remain accepted under the existing setting limits. New captures and Center Pivot share the geometric bounds center (x + w/2, y + h/2), so odd-sized centered parts do not drift on quarter turns; saved pivots are reopened unchanged. The memory ledger includes retained part raster/mask bytes, bounded parameter-history estimates and a suspended practice-session part/history. Canvas/staging/scratch checks are conservative CPU raster estimates, not a measured total heap or latency guarantee. Preview reads existing registry entries without creating blank buffers. A dedicated motion worker admits one active request and one replaceable queued preview, with a 10-second deadline, owned scratch reservations and disposal on session exit/invalid source. Worker and fallback responses contain the rotated part rectangle plus integer destination offsets, with fractional sampling phase retained; preview and bake clip and stamp that rectangle. The controller validates dimensions, offsets and byte length against the initiating request. Scratch admission scales with the captured and rotated rectangles; full-canvas composition and staged cels retain separate admission. The synchronous full-canvas transform API remains a compatibility adapter. Its failure/cancellation never terminates the separate heavy-math dispatcher. Without workers, synchronous work is limited to 1,048,576 estimated units; larger requests refuse. Composition and per-cel staging still use the main thread. UI Bake renders poses asynchronously and validates the initiating store/source before atomic publication; Cancel or changes to document, source, selection or motion ownership refuse unfinished work; autosave cleanliness and view-only zoom changes do not cancel an otherwise valid bake. The synchronous engine entry point remains available for deterministic callers/tests. Initial source artwork stays visible until a valid derived preview is ready; subsequent pending requests can retain the last valid preview. Preview uses elapsed time and the source cel duration, matching copied baked holds. Playback advances only after the current worker pose has presented; slow computation may skip displayed poses without invalidating every response. During motion, the onion canvas is cleared and hidden; closing the session redraws neighboring ghosts. Preview counts nontransparent raster samples outside the document, ignoring transparent rotated bounding padding; this is a crop warning for the current pose, not a guarantee of arbitrary-angle detail conservation. Parameter Undo/Redo stores at most 50 settings snapshots per direction, with gesture grouping, cancellation and source validation; it never copies pixels or falls through to drawing history during the session. Source-rebind UI, skeletons, deforming regions, foot/contact locking, normal-vector qualification and artist quality studies remain future work. [Follow-up measurements and qualification (local archive)](../archives.md#record-docs-research-2026-10-07-editing-quality-release-hardening-md).

The explicit **Merge artwork & animate** preparation composites the current frame's visible color layers into a newly owned layer, preserving their native opacity/blend appearance. It refuses visible locked or dimension-mismatched buffers, any normal-map layer, a floating selection, an active motion session or co-draw. Hidden layers retain their buffer ownership. One structural Undo step and `commitWorkspace` publish the staged registry/metadata atomically; failure restores store, buffers and history. Capture then covers the complete merged drawing regardless of the previous selection. Cancel followed by artwork Undo restores the separate layers; a completed bake needs one additional Undo first. Existing **Create Motion Part** keeps its active-layer/selection semantics.

Motion currently accepts color artwork in a solo workspace. Normal-map sources and co-draw refuse capture/bake; saved-rig editing also refuses co-draw. Moving parts also refuse frames containing normal artwork or research siblings that reference the moving source as color/normal input, because those siblings cannot yet move with the part. Identity arcs may copy valid sibling provenance. Existing sibling buffers must match the document dimensions; an unallocated sibling remains a legitimate empty cel. Commit or cancel floating artwork before capturing, opening or baking a motion part. A transient ownership flag exposes the viewport's floating-selection state; it is not serialized. Explicit document resets clear viewport-local floating artwork even when the project ID and dimensions stay the same. Free-transform commit/cancel validates ownership captured after lifting, including project, dimensions, source frame and buffer generation/revision. A stale Cancel does not create the old layer, overwrite newer pixels or clear another document's selection.

During a motion session, older selection keyboard handlers yield to the motion owner. Motion shortcuts yield to dialogs, editable controls, buttons, composition and modifier-owned commands; Tab retains native focus navigation. Enter on a focused Cancel button activates Cancel without also baking. Canvas arrows adjust the pose without lifting or editing the authored source.

Batch cleanup and sampled-color erase match the active layer to one uniquely named counterpart with the same generated tag in every frame. They never use stack position as correspondence. Missing or ambiguous counterparts refuse the entire batch before pixel changes; use This frame or give counterparts matching unique names. Locked matching layers remain skipped. Persistent layer tracks are not implemented by this name-based safety policy.

Frame duplication copies semantic palette regions to the new frame with fresh binding IDs, subject to the existing binding limit. History restores these bindings with the frame; deletion prunes frame-bound bindings undoably. Copied research recipes fork references to sources/normals within the duplicated frame, retaining their pixel hashes; references outside that frame retain their original provenance.

Legacy `LayerEffectRecipe` describes provenance with generic bounded parameters; only supported tools reopen/recompute it. Research recipes have their own parser, bounds and source/normal SHA-256 fingerprints. Loading, transforming or merging can detach provenance when it no longer describes one source. Older readers can omit optional fields.

Try a new look supports bounded protected simplification, shared light and small scenes: at most 256 px/axis, 16 frames, 262,144 source pixels; at most 16 tiles / 20,000 search states for scenes. Original remains unchanged before Apply. Worker result publication checks ownership/generation/source freshness and commits ready buffers, document metadata and one structural history step before notifying observers. Cancel, stale results and refusal must not replace artwork.

| --- | --- |
| GIF | Transfers owned snapshots; missing-worker/initialization fallback uses still-owned input. Failure after transfer rejects; detached arrays or freshly changed artwork are not used for a silent retry |
| Heavy worker math (EDT normal-map Bake, rotation, morph; internal WFC retained) | Dispatcher deadlines/cancellation, at most four pending requests and a 10-second default deadline; fallback is bounded to 1,048,576 estimated work units and can block the UI. | Animation review | Bounded scan worker, source freshness and intentional exceptions; metrics can flag deliberate changes |
| Research | Owned bounded worker and deadline; stale/cancelled output refused; recipes retain source fingerprints |
