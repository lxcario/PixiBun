## Fixes

- **MP4 offered where it cannot encode.** Firefox reports H.264 as a supported WebCodecs configuration and then fails every encode, and its MediaRecorder records no MP4. So the Export dialog offered MP4 and the export failed. `probeMp4ExportSupport` now encodes one 16 × 16 frame before claiming support, and caches the result per size and frame rate. Where neither the trial encode nor MediaRecorder MP4 works, the dialog explains that MP4 is unavailable and disables the download. `animatedExportIntegrity.spec.ts` decides its expectation the same honest way: an engine that encodes must download a playable file with both poses; one that cannot must refuse explicitly. Both assertions are unchanged.
- **Island shortcut moved when pressed.** The Dynamic island's quick shortcut has its own press on its face, and its hit target must stay put. The global elastic press (16 October) also scaled the button, so `dynamicIsland.spec.ts` "hover shortcut splits…" measured a 0.31–0.56 px shift and failed in 2 of 4 runs. The button is now marked `data-no-press`, as AGENTS.md requires for controls that must not move; 8 of 8 repeats passed.
- **Workflow pinning.** The autofix workflow's actions, including `anthropics/claude-code-action`, are pinned to commit SHAs like the rest of CI. `tests/workflowPinning.test.ts` now fails if any workflow uses a moving tag, lacks a `permissions` block, or has a job without `timeout-minutes`.

## Found by the complete browser suite

The complete production suite had not run since the 15–16 October interface work. Running it exposed three product defects and several tests that still described earlier interfaces.

**Product defects**

- **Curve re-edit never worked.** Since `6da38209` (16 October, smoother painting), a stroke publishes its store update on the next animation frame. Curve's Apply records the workspace revision immediately to allow Re-edit, so the late update made every committed curve look stale. "Re-edit Curve" silently dismissed itself in every engine (`curveColorRetention` ×2 and `curveOwnership` "immediate re-edit" failed in Chromium and Firefox). `commitSnapshot` now publishes any pending update when a stroke is committed; strokes still batch while they are drawn. `tests/strokeCommitRevision.test.tsx` fails without the change.
- **Tools cut off on small laptops.** The tool shelf folds groups by predicting its height, but its model still assumed the 36 px buttons used before the 16 October icon change made them 40 px. At 1366 × 657, even fully folded, the Eyedropper slot sat 7 px below the shelf. The model now uses the measured 40 px. A final Compact stage returns buttons to 36 px only when nothing else fits (`src/lib/layout/toolShelf.ts`); larger windows are unchanged. The plan counts a fit when the last button shows: the shelf's 8 px bottom padding may be clipped, as it already was at 1280 × 720. A first version that also reserved the padding folded Normal Chisel and the Eyedropper together at 1280 × 720, which `chiselAndPaletteLiveProof.spec.ts` caught. A unit case pins the measured 347 px full-size height and both window sizes.
- **Hydration mismatch under reduced motion.** The homepage's pixel wave divider chose its first animation frame from `useReducedMotion()`, which is unknown on the server, so a reduced-motion visitor's page failed hydration for those elements. The first render is now identical everywhere, and reduced motion settles instantly once in view. A sweep of `/`, `/playground`, `/studio`, `/pledge`, `/open` and `/verified` found no other hydration errors in either motion mode.

- **Light bar segments trailed behind and hid their choice.** In the light bar (`411d2b8e`), each Shade, Glint and Material segment drew its highlight as a Motion shared-layout thumb, positioned from its place on screen. Opening **More** grows the bar and moves it up, so each thumb animated in from where it had been: measured 31 px below its button, crawling back over about 0.8 s here, and visibly longer on a busier machine. Meanwhile the selected label, dark text meant for the orange highlight, nearly vanished. Each segment now has one lens placed from offsets inside the segment, so the bar moving never displaces it; measured 0 px off its button throughout the drawer opening. Choosing an option springs the lens over and stretches it with its speed, like the tool rail's liquid lens (51 → 58 px wide mid-glide, with one small overshoot). With the Liquid controls lab on, the segments also take the rail's gesture: press and hold (170 ms) or slide past 5 px, and the lens lifts into glass, follows the pointer with a rubber band past the ends and magnifies the option under it; releasing chooses that option. Taps and the keyboard are unchanged. Releasing chooses the option shown magnified under the lens; a first version chose from the lens's own position, which trails the pointer, so a quick slide could land on the previous option. The lens is glass only while held: it turns solid within 140 ms of release and springs into place, rather than staying glass until the spring fully rests (close to a second). `liveGlint.spec.ts` covers hold, slide, release and tap.

- **Frames list structure.** The timeline's Frames list (`role="list"`) ended in the "Animate / New frame" button directly, which axe reports as a critical `aria-required-children` violation: assistive technology announces a list whose items are not all list items. The slot is now its own list item; its look and behaviour are unchanged.

- **Frame card actions below the target size.** The 16 October per-frame Duplicate and Delete buttons were 20 × 20 px; axe flagged them in the Studio audit. They are 24 × 24 px, the WCAG 2.2 minimum, and still fit side by side on a 76 px card.
- **Web analytics outside Vercel.** An uncommitted change in the working tree (17 October) added Vercel Web Analytics to the root layout for every environment. In development it loaded a debug script from another host, which the page CSP refuses, so every page logged an error (`verified.spec.ts`). Locally it would post to an endpoint that does not exist. It now renders only on Vercel deployments, where script and endpoint are same-origin. The privacy guide and security boundaries describe it: cookieless page views, with no artwork, project or diagnostic content.

- **Cocoa Day contrast.** In the Cocoa theme's Day palette (added 16 October), accent text such as the light bar's "2 shadow tones" measured 4.48:1 on the inspector's sunken panel, under the 4.5:1 AA minimum. The accent moves from `#9a4416` to `#954215` (4.69:1), the same hue; `themes.generated.css` is regenerated and `tests/themeRegistry.test.ts` still passes.

- **Making-of receipt miscounted strokes.** `cartPlayer.spec.ts` counted 1 stroke instead of 3 in Chromium. A first fix queued pictures taken while the Studio switches drawings, which had been discarded until the previous drawing's ledger was saved (`src/lib/cart/receiptRecorder.ts`). The next run then counted **4** strokes in one of two Firefox and WebKit repeats. Logging the recorder in a production build showed why: it pictures the drawing once edits go quiet for 350 ms, and a stroke publishes as it is drawn, so a slow stroke (WebKit took over 400 ms from press to release for a short line) was pictured half drawn and counted twice. Pictures now wait while a pointer is held and are taken after the release, or when the window loses focus. `tests/receiptRecorder.test.ts` fails without this. The logs also showed the test drawing before its `?width=16` drawing had opened (the Studio reports active first); it now waits for the drawing to open. The first fix is kept: the logs show a picture arriving during a switch. Whether the Chromium undercount came from that or from the test's race was not established.
